# Cyber Threat Intelligence Definition (CTI)
The primary objective of our CTI team is to transition our defensive strategies from merely reactive measures to more proactive, anticipatory stance.

Four fundamental principles make CTI an integral part of our cyber security strategy:
![[Pasted image 20260705235550.png]]
- **Relevance**: How relevant is information to our organisation
- **Timeliness**: Switch communication of intelligence is crucial for the implementation of effective mitigation measures.
- **Actionability**:  Data under analysis by a CTI analyst should yield actionable insights
- **Accuracy**: Before disseminating any intelligence, it must be verified for accuracy.
# Criteria of Cyber Threat Intelligence 
- For CTI to be effective it must be **Actionable**, **Timely**, **Relevant** and **Accurate**.
- Allows businesses to minimize risk as much as possible.

As information is compiled, it transforms into intelligence. Which can then be classified into three different categories:
- **Strategic Intelligence**
	- Being consumed by C-suite executives, VPs, and other company leaders
	- Aiming to align intelligence directly with company risks to inform decisions
	- Providing an overview of the adversary's operations over time
	- Mapping TTPs and Modus Operandi (MO) of the adversary
	- Striving to answer the Who? and Why?
- **Operational Intelligence**
	- Also including TTPs of an adversary (similar to strategic intelligence)
	- Providing information on adversary campaigns
	- Offering more detail than what's found in strategic intelligence reports
	- Being produced for mid-level management personnel
	- Working towards answering the How? and Where?
- **Tactical Intelligence**
	-  Delivering immediate actionable information
	- Being provided to network defenders for swift action
	- Including technical details on attacks that have occurred or could occur in the near future
# How to go through a Tactical Threat Intelligence Report
1. Comprehending the Report's Scope and Narrative
2. Spotting and Classifying the IOCs
3. Comprehending the Attack's Lifecycle
4. Analysis and Validation of IOCs
5. Incorporating the IOCs into our Security Infrastructure
6. Proactive Threat Hunting
7. Continuous Monitoring and Learning
